Automation

AI · Report automation

I moved the inventory checks, reports and settlement people used to repeat into six automations.

GoalMove repeated checking, reporting and settlement work into systems

Problem
As repeat checks piled up, gathering data took longer than finding what was actually wrong.
What I did
I automated the work, but first set the stop conditions so it runs only when the data is sound.
Result
I automated inventory, reporting and monitoring work. Bad data is blocked before anything runs, and results are confirmed through follow-up checks and records.

01

Problem

It started as a way to cut one-by-one manual checks and make time for decisions.

Too much work depended on someone remembering to check. Checking late meant wasted ad spend; spending time on checks left no time for decisions.

02

What the data showed

Even data from a half-finished collection could switch off ads all at once.

I started by counting what could go wrong. Even if inventory collection only half succeeds, the decision logic still runs. Then products that are still in stock read as sold out and their ads get switched off. The danger wasn't the system stopping — it was the system carrying on.

03

Key decision

Ads change only when the data passes every soundness condition.

I put safeguards ahead of speed. If any one of these is detected — a missing reference file, zero samples, too high a share of unknown stock, or too high a collection failure rate — the job stops without touching ads and alerts the person in charge.

04

Execution

  • Shopping-search inventory automation — scheduled collection, pausing and restarting ads by stock level, up to 900 products per run
  • Report automation engine — paste three RAW files to generate the dashboard, campaign report and QC automatically, with 38 media aliases and 82 audience groups mapped automatically
  • Three automated anomaly monitors — dead landing pages, spend pacing, and scheduled daily GA4 comments for 6 media
  • Ad-spend settlement automation — corporate-card receipts registered automatically in e-approval, all cases processed, up to 84 a month (2026.05)
  • Automated monthly report delivery — 12 core metrics aligned to a common basis, sent as PDFs automatically (Python · PostgreSQL · Docker)
  • Two work-knowledge bots — in-house chatbots that search 854 files of work records and answer with the source files

07

Failures and lessons

Because stop conditions came first, I never had to undo a bad run while operating it.

What didn't work

At first I looked at how fast it ran. Then I hit a case where the decision ran when collection was only half done. Left as it was, even products that were selling fine would all have been switched off at once. After that I changed the order. Now it first checks that collection was complete, and if that can't be confirmed, it doesn't touch the ads.

What I learned

Deciding where to stop bought more time in the end than making it faster. While I ran the accounts after building it, I didn't have to undo anything after a bad run. But I haven't counted manual interventions yet, so this isn't a confirmed value that there were none over the whole period.
