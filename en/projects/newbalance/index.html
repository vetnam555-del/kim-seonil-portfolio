01

Problem

When stock changed, nothing told the ad side.

Even with ROAS that looked good, showing sold-out products or letting spend stall cost money. When I took over, the owner opened the site page by page to check stock, and doing it by hand led to frequent errors. Internal checks took about 2 hours a day; the public MOP case study of the same task put the manual average at about 3 hours. I started automating so the time saved could go into finding insights.

02

What the data showed

While stock-outs went unnoticed, ad spend kept going out.

Before cutting check time, I counted what we were missing. When people check hundreds of SKUs across 10 channels from memory, stock-outs and stalled spend were always noticed a beat late. What mattered more than average time was when a problem gets noticed. So I set the goal of the automation on detection time, not speed.

03

Key decision

I put inventory into ad decisions, and made sure ads are left untouched when the data looks wrong.

I didn't judge budget on media ROAS alone; I split the operating rules to look at stock status, search demand and each channel's role together. I blocked duplicate runs, stale data and matching errors first, and made the system stop before touching ads when abnormal data came in.

05

Execution

  • Scheduled inventory collection → threshold-based decisions → media API execution (up to 900 per run) → sheet log and post-run QA → alerts only when action is needed
  • Daily and weekly reports generated through to dashboards and QC just by pasting RAW data
  • Automatic monitoring of dead landing pages and spend-pacing anomalies, alongside LG CNS MOP–based Shopping Search bid optimization
  • Safety first — automatic actions stop and the owner is alerted when inventory data looks wrong
  • Keyword expansion split into source check → exclusion rules → candidates → priority review; nothing is called a result before it's actually registered and approved

Deciding 'when to stop' first

The most dangerous failure in automation wasn't stopping; it was running on with wrong data. So I set the stop conditions before the execution steps.

  1. 01

    ① Collect

    Inventory data is pulled on a fixed cycle. If this step fails, no ad changes or sheet logs happen.

  2. 02

    ② Consistency check

    Checks that row counts, duplicates and the reference date are in the expected range. If not, it stops without touching ads.

  3. 03

    ③ Decide

    Picks which ads to pause or restart based on stock levels. Up to 900 products per run.

  4. 04

    ④ Execute · log

    Runs through the media API and logs to a sheet. Calls the owner only when action is needed.

If it stops at ②, it ends with 0 changes. Normal runs aren't reported, so there's no alert fatigue.

07

My role

Running the 10 channels was a team effort. I planned, built and ran the inventory automation, report engine and monitoring systems on my own, and every system is documented in a handover package.

Did myself

  • All planning, building and running of the inventory automation, report engine and automated checks
  • Checked the 18,396-row source file and set the rules for choosing expansion candidates
  • Wrote a rulebook for budget decisions by channel type
  • Daily and weekly commentary standards and advertiser communication
  • Wrote creative briefs — set placements and messages by product group and goal, then passed them on as production requests

Did with others

  • Media operations across the 10 channels were split within the team
  • Shopping Search bid optimization ran together with the LG CNS MOP solution

Not my part

  • Total brand revenue and offline channels were outside my scope
  • I wasn't involved in actual production such as creative design or photography, or in product planning

ContributionAccount operations 40% · automation design & build 100%

08

Failures and lessons

There was a ROAS of 13,846%, but it had no cost behind it, so I didn't use it as a reason to raise budget.

What didn't work

Looking only at the past Powerlink-attributed ROAS of 13,846.66%, it seemed safe to expand right away. But the Brand Search source had no cost, and no incremental effect could be confirmed. So I didn't use the number as a Brand Search result or a reason to raise budget, and limited it to a reference for choosing candidates.

What I learned

Scheduled runs alone weren't enough to trust with stock-outs. I checked the timing and scope of the collected data and logged each result so the next check could compare against it.
