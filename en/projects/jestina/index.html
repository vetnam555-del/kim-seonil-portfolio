01

Problem

Revenue was concentrated in search, and the display ads (DA) meant to bring in new customers ran separately at each agency.

Revenue was there, but growth had stalled. DA was already running, but each channel sat with a different agency, and the work stopped at making and uploading creatives. With no one looking across channels, no combined insight came out, and most revenue sat in search, where customers who already knew the brand came in.

02

What the data showed

Looking at repeat and new customers separately, search was mostly bringing back people who already knew the brand.

Once the scattered channels ran in one place, I could see what had been invisible: how keywords and creatives related, and even viral and external factors moving outside the ad platforms, all on one screen. I cross-checked GA4 against media data to separate repeat customers from new visitors. Search was strong at capturing repeat purchases but contributed little to new demand. Ranking channels on efficiency alone gave no reason to move budget.

Creative planning

03

Key decision

I gave each stage — awareness, consideration, conversion — its own channels and KPIs.

First I put operations under one owner so learning could build across channels. Then I split the budget by customer stage: DA took Awareness, Shopping and Search took Consideration, and Brand Search and Search took Conversion, each with its own KPI.

The order I split the budget in

Before moving budget by channel efficiency, I brought operations under one owner, separated customers, and then decided what each stage should do.

  1. Step 1: Consolidate DA

    Ran GFA and Kakao Moment, previously scattered across agencies, in one place

  2. Step 2: Separate customers

    Cross-checked GA4 with media data to separate repeat customers from new visitors

  3. Step 3: Stage roles & KPIs

    Assigned channels and KPIs separately to Awareness, Consideration and Conversion

05

Execution

  • Brought GFA and Kakao Moment, previously split across agencies, into one place so learning built across channels
  • Set up an analysis routine that reads keyword–creative correlation together with viral and external factors, and split creative messages by customer group
  • Built a UTM Builder to put channels, creatives and landing pages on one analysis standard
  • Rebuilt Search into full-funnel monitoring — keyword expansion, non-impression management, T&D and landing checks
  • Automated a new report template and media–GA4 cross-reporting

Which way the budget moved

Sorting channels by efficiency kept pushing budget into search. Search mostly captures people who already know the brand, so new demand didn't grow. So I switched the basis from channel to customer stage.

07

My role

I owned consolidating the separately run DA channels, defining customer segments, channel roles, UTM standards and the measurement system. Day-to-day execution was shared with the team.

Did myself

  • Proposed and carried out bringing GFA and Kakao Moment, split across agencies, into one place
  • Defined customer segments and designed each channel's role and KPI
  • Designed the 5-level UTM Builder parameter system and applied it across all channels
  • Set the media–GA4 cross-reporting standard and attribution rules
  • Wrote creative briefs and a banner-spec guide per media — kept as a shared team standard

Did with others

  • Daily execution and creative uploads were shared with the team

Not my part

  • Creative design and photography belong to the production team
  • Offline stores and home-shopping channels were outside my scope

08

Failures and lessons

Ranked by channel efficiency alone, the budget kept going to customers who already knew the brand.

What didn't work

At first I tried to move budget in order of channel ROAS. Budget kept flowing to search, and search mostly brings back people who already know the brand, so new demand didn't grow. Once I saw that an efficiency ranking gave no reason to move budget, I switched to thinking in customer stages.

What I learned

When channels are handed to different owners, each runs its own well, but no one sees the whole. Only after bringing them into one place could I connect keywords, creatives and even outside factors — and only then was there a basis for moving budget.
