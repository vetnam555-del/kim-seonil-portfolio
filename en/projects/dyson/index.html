01

Problem

More budget was going to display (DP) ads, which were performing worse.

Premium appliances are high-involvement products with long research before purchase. But subscriber growth, content viewing and live-commerce traffic were each run separately, so each campaign's role and the next action were unclear. In early February, I also found more budget going to display (DP) ads, which were performing worse.

02

What the data showed

On the same basis, video had a higher CTR and a lower CPC than DP.

The December 2023 subscription campaign recorded 104,257 conversions at a CPA of about KRW 1,132. Comparing formats in February 2024 showed a clear gap: DP at CTR 0.73% and CPC KRW 637, video at CTR 2.16% and CPC KRW 160. I used this comparison as the basis for reallocating budget.

03

Key decision

I set separate success measures for subscriptions, live and content, and compared ad formats on the same yardstick.

First I redefined what counts as performance. Views alone couldn't tell whether the thumbnail or the content was weak, so I split impressions, product clicks and 10-second-plus views into separate metrics. Then I separated the purpose and KPI of subscription conversion, content learning and live traffic. I compared formats over the same period and metrics, and concentrated budget on video once its efficiency was confirmed. Community posts carried the broadcast benefit and product name so viewers moved on to join the live.

05

Execution

  • Defined impressions, product clicks and 10-second-plus views as separate metrics (in the platform report: Impression / Product Click Frequency / TTL Viewers)
  • Switched the order: check popular keywords first, then design titles and content around them
  • Treated the home feed, playlists and community as owned inventory and redesigned placement rules (PC and mobile separately)
  • Designed a test that splits three variables — thumbnail, playlist, community — into three combinations in 10-day blocks
  • Separated campaign purposes and KPIs into subscription conversion, live reservations and content learning
  • Compared February DP vs video CTR and CPC, and shifted weight toward video from February 23
  • Ran the production schedule for 53 videos, Shorts, thumbnails and community posts a month with the production team
  • Planned community quizzes and notices using the live broadcast benefits and product names
  • Separated algorithm-learning periods from stable periods and judged by trend rather than short-term swings

What counts as performance · KPI redefinition

Views alone couldn't show whether the channel was becoming an asset. I separated a new user's first click from actual viewing, and set impressions, interest and viewing as different metrics.

MetricWhen it countsThe question it answers
ImpressionTimes a thumbnail was shown in YouTube inventoryIs our content getting chances to be seen?

If clicks and viewing are one metric, a thumbnail problem can't be told apart from a content problem. Splitting them decides what to fix next.

Keywords before content

I reversed the order of making a video and then attaching keywords. I checked search demand first and built titles and content around it.

  1. 01

    AS-IS · make the video

    Made it first, then attached keywords that seemed to fit.

  2. 02

    Check popular keywords first

    Looked at real search demand in channel analytics and the Ideas tab first.

  3. 03

    Redesign titles and content

    Rewrote titles in the words people actually search for, not product names.

  4. 04

    TO-BE · keyword-led production

    Took the next video's topic itself from search demand.

Example: 'A bob-haired show host's morning commute' → 'A show host explains how to use the Corrale hair straightener'. The proposed keyword had +235% search volume compared with the one in the old title. This is the search volume I checked when choosing the keyword (channel analytics and the Ideas tab).

What a 1M-view goal newly requires · H2 improvement plan

Strengthening existing content alone showed a reach ceiling. I proposed, by quarter, one axis that strengthens what works now and one that opens categories we didn't have. This, too, is a second-half plan written after the campaign ended.

  1. 01

    Strengthen · what works now

    Answer product questions with new-product reviews and seasonal benefits, and order demos by interest.

  2. 02

    Expand · care by condition

    Content by hair and scalp condition could be made by re-editing, without new shoots.

  3. 03

    New · tutorials

    Opened new categories in units people search for, such as men's partings, women's bobs and long hair.

  4. 04

    Expand · formats and audiences

    Proposed pulling a 2-minute main video and 30- and 15-second Shorts from one plan, and linking with the makeup category.

'Strengthen' is a conclusion from measured top content; 'expand' and 'new' are pre-execution proposals.

08

Failures and lessons

Put subscription and sales campaigns in one ranking, and both get misread.

What didn't work

In early February DP had the larger share, but video actually had better CTR and CPC. I also found that mixing a temporary rise during March's learning period, or swings from April's budget and creative changes, with stable-period results could lead to the wrong conclusion.

What I learned

I should separate campaign purposes first, compare formats on one basis, and only then move budget. Separating learning and stable periods, and running content, community and live as one flow, let me manage channel assets and short-term results together.
